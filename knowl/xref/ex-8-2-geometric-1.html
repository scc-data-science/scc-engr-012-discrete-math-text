Example 8.2.5.

Let \(a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots\) be the sequence defined recursively as follows. Let:
\begin{equation*} a_k = 2 \cdot a_{k - 1} \end{equation*}
for \(k \geq 1\) together with the initial condition \(a_0 = 3\text{.}\) Use iteration to deduce an explicit formula for the sequence.
🔗
Hint.
Start by writing \(a_1\text{,}\) then substitute \(a_1\) into the expression for \(a_2\text{.}\) Repeat this process for as many \(a_k\) as you need until you start to notice a pattern.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Let’s write out the first few terms of the sequence.
\begin{align*} a_0 \amp = 3, \\ a_1 \amp = 2 \cdot a_0 = 2 \cdot 3, \\ a_2 \amp = 2 \cdot a_1 = 2 \cdot (2 \cdot 3) = 2^2 \cdot 3, \\ a_3 \amp = 2 \cdot a_2 = 2 \cdot (2^2 \cdot 3) = 2^3 \cdot 3. \end{align*}
This time, we deliberately did not simplify all the way before moving on to the next term in the sequence. In general, it is beneficial to leave things unsimplified while you are searching for patterns, as this problem demonstrates.
🔗
In this case, we see that the pattern appears to be:
\begin{equation*} a_k = 3 \cdot 2^{k}. \end{equation*}
Indeed, \(a_3 = 3 \cdot 2^{3}\text{,}\) as expected, so this formula seems to be doing the right thing as well.
