Theorem 7.5.3 Law of Total Probability.

Let \(B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_n\) be a partition of the sample space. Then the probability of event \(A\) can be written as:
\begin{equation*} \mathbb{P}(A) = \sum_{k=1}^n \mathbb{P}(A \cap B_k) = \sum_{k=1}^n \mathbb{P}(A \mid B_k) \mathbb{P}(B_k). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

If \(B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_n\) is a partition of the sample space, then:
\begin{equation*} \bigcup_{k = 1}^m B_k = \Omega, \end{equation*}
where \(B_k \cap B_{\ell} = \varnothing\) for any \(k \neq \ell\) in \(\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}\text{.}\) Then,
\begin{align*} \mathbb{P}(A) \amp = \mathbb{P}(A \cap \Omega) \\ \amp = \mathbb{P}\left(A \cap \bigcup_{k = 1}^m B_k\right) \\ \amp = \sum_{k=1}^n \mathbb{P}(A \cap B_k). \end{align*}
The rest follows from Definition 7.3.1.
