Example 5.4.7.

Find the remainder when \(2^{314}\) is divided by \(5\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Here’s an example of a type of number with which you cannot reasonably use the division algorithm. \(2^{314} \approx 10^{94}\text{,}\) which is far too large to deal with by hand in regular settings. However, with modular arithmetic, it is much more manageable. Let’s look at the first few powers of \(2\) modulo 5:
🔗
\begin{align*} 2^1 \amp = 2 \equiv 2 \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ 2^2 \amp = 4 \equiv 4 \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ 2^3 \amp = 8 \equiv 3 \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ 2^4 \amp = 16 \equiv 1 \amp (\text{mod } 5) \end{align*}
🔗
So, \(2^4 \equiv 1 \, (\text{mod } 5)\text{!}\) We can use this to make our problem much, much easier. Notice that \(314 = 4 \cdot 78 + 2\text{,}\) and so if \(x \equiv 2^{314} \, (\text{mod }5)\text{,}\) then:
🔗
\begin{align*} x \amp\equiv 2^{314} \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ \amp\equiv 2^{4 \cdot 78 + 2} \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ \amp\equiv 2^{4 \cdot 78} \cdot 2^{2} \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ \amp\equiv (2^{4})^{78} \cdot 2^{2} \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ \amp\equiv (1)^{78} \cdot 2^{2} \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ \amp\equiv 2^{2} \amp (\text{mod } 5) \\ \amp\equiv 4 \amp (\text{mod } 5) \end{align*}
🔗
And so \(2^{314}\) has remainder \(4\) when divided by \(5\text{.}\)
