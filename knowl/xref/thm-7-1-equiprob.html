Theorem 7.1.3 Equiprobable Outcomes.

Suppose a sample space \(\Omega\) has \(n\) equally likely outcomes, and an event \(A\) contains \(k\) outcomes. If:
\begin{equation*} \mathbb{P}(A) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes in $A$}}{\text{Number of outcomes in the sample space}} = \frac{\lvert A \rvert}{\lvert \Omega \rvert} = \frac{k}{n}, \end{equation*}
then \(\mathbb{P}\) is a valid probability measure on \(\Omega\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.

  1. An event \(A\) is a subset of the sample space. Since \(A\) can contain no fewer than zero elements, \(\lvert A \rvert \geq 0\text{,}\) and so \(\mathbb{P}(A) \geq 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Suppose \(A\) and \(B\) are disjoint events. Then \(A \cap B = \varnothing\text{.}\) Then, we can think of \(A = \{a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n\}\) and \(B = \{b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_m\}\text{,}\) where none of the \(a_i\)’s are equal to any of the \(b_i\)’s. Then:
    \begin{align*} \mathbb{P}(A) =\amp \frac{n}{\lvert \Omega \rvert} \\ \mathbb{P}(B) =\amp \frac{m}{\lvert \Omega \rvert} \\ \mathbb{P}(A \cup B) =\amp \frac{n + m}{\lvert \Omega \rvert} \end{align*}
    So, \(\mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B) = \frac{n + m}{\lvert \Omega \rvert} = \mathbb{P}(A \cup B)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. By definition, \(\mathbb{P}(\Omega) = \frac{\lvert \Omega \rvert}{\lvert \Omega \rvert} = 1\text{.}\)
