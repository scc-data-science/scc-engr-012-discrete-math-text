Example 7.7.3 Flipping a Fair Coin Three Times.

What is the expected number of heads you would observe when flipping a fair coin three times?
🔗
Hint.
We computed the distribution in the previous section!
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Let \(X\) denote the number of heads we observe. Then, our task is to find \(\mathbb{E}[X]\text{.}\) From our introductory paragraph of Section 7.6, we know that:
\begin{align*} \mathbb{P}(X = 0) \amp = \frac{1}{8} \\ \mathbb{P}(X = 1) \amp = \frac{3}{8} \\ \mathbb{P}(X = 2) \amp = \frac{3}{8} \\ \mathbb{P}(X = 3) \amp = \frac{1}{8} \end{align*}
Then, applying Definition 7.7.2, we have:
\begin{align*} \mathbb{E}[X] \amp = \sum_{k=1}^4 x_k \cdot \mathbb{P}(X = x_k) \\ \amp = 0 \cdot \mathbb{P}(X = 0) + 1 \cdot \mathbb{P}(X = 1) + 2 \cdot \mathbb{P}(X = 2) + 3 \cdot \mathbb{P}(X = 3) \\ \amp = 1 \cdot \frac{3}{8} + 2 \cdot \frac{3}{8} + 3 \cdot \frac{1}{8} \\ \amp = \frac{3}{2}. \end{align*}
Thus, the expected number of heads we should expect to see is 1.5.
