Theorem 5.1.5.

The only divisors of \(1\) are \(1\) and \(-1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Note that \(1 = 1 \cdot 1\text{,}\) and so \(1 \mid 1\text{.}\) Similarly, \(1 = (-1) \cdot (-1)\text{,}\) and so \(-1 \mid 1\text{.}\)
🔗
Now, suppose \(d\) is another integer that divides \(1\text{.}\) Then, there exists an integer \(k\) such that \(1 = kd\text{.}\) Note that since \(1\) is positive, \(k\) and \(d\) are either both positive or both negative. Assume without loss of generality that both \(k\) and \(d\) are positive (the argument would be identical if they were both negative; I would encourage you to think about why). Hence, \(d\) is positive, and therefore, by Lemma 5.1.4, \(d \leq 1\text{.}\) Since \(d\) is a positive integer, we conclude \(d = 1\text{.}\)
