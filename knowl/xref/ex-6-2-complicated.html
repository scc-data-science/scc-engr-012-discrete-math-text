Example 6.2.7.

Suppose that, for your degree, you need to take \(n_1\) math classes, \(n_2\) computer science classes, and \(n_3\) statistics classes. Suppose for the sake of simplicity that you can take any class in any order (i.e., there are no prerequisites). How many different schedules are there such that the classes from a given discipline are contiguous?
🔗
Solution.
We break this problem into steps, where we first examine the possible orderings of the disciplines (e.g., computer science, then math, then stats, vs math, then stats, then computer science), and then we examine the possible ordering of classes within each discipline.
🔗
There are \(3!\) orderings of the disciplines. Within each order, there are \(n_1!\) possible orderings of math classes, \(n_2!\) possible orderings of computer science classes, and \(n_3!\) possible orderings of statistics classes.
🔗
Notice:
  1. the ordering of the disciplines did not affect the ordering of the math classes;
    🔗
    🔗
  2. the ordering of the math classes did not affect the ordering of the computer science classes;
    🔗
    🔗
  3. the ordering of the computer science classes did not affect the ordering of the statistics classes.
    🔗
    🔗
So, we can apply Theorem 6.1.1. In total, there are:
\begin{equation*} 3! \cdot n_1! \cdot n_2! \cdot n_3! \end{equation*}
possible schedule orderings.
