Example 5.5.3 Division by \(17\) modulo \(74\).

Use the Euclidean algorithm to find \(17^{-1}\) modulo \(74\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Step 1: Run the Euclidean algorithm:
🔗
\begin{align*} 74 \amp = 17 \cdot 4 + 6 \\ 17 \amp = 6 \cdot 2 + 5 \\ 6 \amp = 5 \cdot 1 + 1 \end{align*}
🔗
Step 2: Solve each equation above for the remainders, starting from the top. Let \(a = 74\) and \(b = 17\text{.}\) Then:
🔗
\begin{alignat*}{3} 6 \amp = 74 - 17 \cdot 4 \amp\amp = a - 4b. \amp\amp \\ 5 \amp = 17 - 6 \cdot 2 \amp\amp = b - (a - 4b) \cdot 2 \amp\amp = 9b - 2a. \\ 1 \amp = 6 - 5 \cdot 1 \amp\amp = (a - 4b) - (9b - 2a) \cdot 1 \amp\amp = 3a - 13b. \end{alignat*}
🔗
Step 3: Reduce modulo \(a = 74\text{.}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} 3a - 13b = 1 \implies -13b \equiv 1 \, (\text{mod } 74) \implies 61b \equiv 1 \, (\text{mod } 74), \end{equation*}
🔗
Since \(-13 \equiv 61\) (mod \(74\)). Thus, \(17^{-1} = 63\) modulo \(74\text{.}\)
