Theorem 7.9.5 The Law of Large Numbers.

Let \(X\) be a random variable with expected value \(\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]\text{,}\) and let \(X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n\) be independent random variables, each with the same distribution as \(X\text{.}\) Then, for any \(\varepsilon > 0\text{,}\) the probability
\begin{equation*} \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\overline{X}_n - \mu\right| > \varepsilon\right) \end{equation*}
can be made as small as we like by taking \(n\) sufficiently large.
