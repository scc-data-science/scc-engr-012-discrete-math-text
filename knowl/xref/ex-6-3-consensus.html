Example 6.3.5.

In how many different ways can the word CONSENSUS be rearranged?
🔗
Solution.
We solved this problem in the previous section (see Example 6.2.5), but we’ll use a sligtly different approach here that utilizes this new notation.
🔗
We imagine having all of the letters of CONSENSUS set aside, while in front of us we have nine empty slots to which we’ll assign each of the letters. One way to do this is to first place the three S’s into any of the nine slots. How many ways are there to do that? There are \(\binom{9}{3}\) many such ways.
🔗
Now that we have placed each of the S’s, we only have \(9 - 3 = 6\) spaces left. Now, how many ways can we place the two N’s? There are \(\binom{6}{2}\) many such ways.
🔗
After placing the N’s, we’re down to \(9 - 3 - 2 = 4\) open slots on the table. For each of the remaining letters, there is only letter from which to choose, so in each case there will be \(\binom{n_i}{1}\) many ways to place that letter, where \(n_i\) denotes the number of open slots left on the table.
🔗
Finally, we can apply Theorem 6.1.1 to put everything together. In total, there are:
\begin{equation*} \binom{9}{3} \cdot \binom{6}{2} \cdot \binom{4}{1} \cdot \binom{3}{1} \cdot \binom{2}{1} \cdot \binom{1}{1} \end{equation*}
many such ways to rearrange the letters of CONSENSUS
