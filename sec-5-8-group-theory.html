    Section 5.8 The Group Theoretic Perspective

    The last few sections have focused on the additive properties of modular arithmetic. In the next few sections, we’ll discuss the multiplicative properties.
    🔗

    Subsection 5.8.1 The Integers Modulo \(m\) as a Set

    To make things easier to work with, we define the following set.
    🔗

    Definition 5.8.1.

    Let \(\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z} = \{0, 1, \ldots, m - 1\}\) be the set of integers modulo \(m\text{,}\) equipped with the following definitions of addition (\(+\)) and multiplication (\(\cdot\)):
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} a + b := a + b \pmod m\\ a \cdot b := a \cdot b \pmod m \end{gather*}
    🔗
    for any \(a, b \in \mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z}\text{.}\) We read \(\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z}\) as “Z mod \(m\) Z” or say "the integers modulo \(m\text{.}\)"
    🔗
    It is common to abbreviate and write \(\mathbb{Z}_m := \mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    In Definition 5.8.1, the \(+\) and \(\cdot\) on the left side of each equality are the \(+\) and \(\cdot\) operators defined in \(\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z}\text{,}\) while the \(+\) and \(\cdot\) on the right side of each equality are the \(+\) and \(\cdot\) operators defined in \(\mathbb{Z}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Example 5.8.2.

    In \(\mathbb{Z} / 7 \mathbb{Z}\text{,}\) what is \(4 + 5\text{?}\) \(4 \cdot 5\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    In \(\mathbb{Z} / 7 \mathbb{Z}\text{,}\) \(4 + 5 = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In \(\mathbb{Z} / 7 \mathbb{Z}\text{,}\) \(4 \cdot 5 = 6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    This set allows us to discuss modular arithmetic without use of congruences and the modulo operator.
    🔗
    We found in previous section that lots of interesting structure arises when you examine numbers in \(\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z}\) that are coprime to the modulus \(m\text{.}\) Motivated by this, we define the following subset of \(\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Definition 5.8.3 The Group of Units Modulo \(m\).

    Let \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times} = \{ a \in \mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z} \; \mid \; \mathrm{gcd}(a, m) = 1 \}\) be the set of elements of \(\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z}\) that are coprime to \(m\text{.}\)
    🔗
    We read this set as “Z mod \(m\) Z star” and occasionally write \(\mathbb{Z}_m^{\times} := (\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 5.8.4.

    Write down the elements of \((\mathbb{Z} / 6 \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    By definition, \((\mathbb{Z} / 6 \mathbb{Z})^{\times} = \{ a \in \mathbb{Z} / 6 \mathbb{Z} \; \mid \; \mathrm{gcd}(a, 6) = 1 \}\text{.}\) Since the factors of \(6\) are \(2\) and \(3\text{,}\) we know \(2\text{,}\) \(3\text{,}\) and \(4\) are not elements of \((\mathbb{Z} / 6 \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\) Therefore, we have \((\mathbb{Z} / 6 \mathbb{Z})^{\times} = \{ 1, 5 \}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 5.8.5.

    For any prime \(p\text{,}\) \((\mathbb{Z} / p \mathbb{Z})^{\times} = \{1, 2, \ldots, p - 1\} = \mathbb{Z} / p \mathbb{Z} \smallsetminus \{0\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Since \(p\) is prime, all positive integers less than \(p\) are coprime to \(p\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    This set, as suggested by the name of Definition 5.8.3, is an example of a mathematical group. Let’s take some time to define groups, prove that this is indeed a group, and discuss the consequences.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 5.8.2 The Group of Units

    Definition 5.8.6.

    A group is a nonempty set \(G\) equipped with a binary operation \(\times\text{,}\) that maps pairs of elements of \(G\) to an element of \(G\text{,}\) that satisfies:
    🔗
    1. Closure: For every \(g_1, g_2 \in G\text{,}\) \(g_1 \cdot g_2 \in G\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Associativity: For every \(g_1, g_2, g_3 \in G\text{,}\) \((g_1 \cdot g_2) \cdot g_3 = g_1 \cdot (g_2 \cdot g_3)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Identity: There exists an element \(e \in G\) such that \(e \cdot g = g \cdot e = g\) for any \(g \in G\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Inverse: For every \(g \in G\text{,}\) there exists \(g' \in G\) such that \(g \cdot g' = g' \cdot g = e\text{,}\) the identity element in \(G\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 5.8.7.

    The set \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\) is a group under multiplication.
    🔗
    That is, \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{,}\) together with the notion of multiplication defined in Definition 5.8.1, is a group as defined by Definition 5.8.6.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    We need to prove the four conditions defined in Definition 5.8.6.
    🔗
    1. Closure: Let \(a, b \in (\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\) Then, by definition, \(\mathrm{gcd}(a, m) = \mathrm{gcd}(b, m) = 1\text{,}\) meaning that neither \(a\) nor \(b\) share any common factors with \(m\text{.}\) It follows that \(a \cdot b\) shares no common factors with \(m\text{.}\) It follows from the division algorithm that \(a \cdot b \pmod{m}\) shares no common factors with \(m\text{,}\) and so \(a \cdot b \in (\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Associativity: This follows from the associativity of multiplication in \(\mathbb{Z}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Identity: For any \(m \gt 1\text{,}\) \(1 \in (\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{,}\) since \(\mathrm{gcd}(1, m) = 1\) independent of \(m\text{.}\) Additionally, \(1 \cdot g = g \cdot 1 = g\) for any \(g \in (\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\) Hence, the idenitity exists and is equal to \(1\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Inverse: Since every element \(a \in (\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\) satisfies \(\mathrm{gcd}(a, m) = 1\text{,}\) every element in \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\) has an inverse by Theorem 5.5.1.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Hence, \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\) is a group under multiplication.
    🔗
    🔗
    Perhaps the most important takeaways are that \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\) is closed under multiplication and that inverses exist for each element.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 5.8.3 Powers of elements of \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\)

    Let’s explore some consequences of the closure of \((\mathbb{Z} / m \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Lets work in \((\mathbb{Z} / 5 \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\) and consider the powers of all of the elements.
    🔗
    There are three interesting results here.
    🔗
    1. The last row is all ones.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Some columns appear to reach \(1\) early and cycle.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. The columns that don’t cycle seem to generate all elements of \((\mathbb{Z} / 5 \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Does this result hold? Consider now \((\mathbb{Z} / 7 \mathbb{Z})^{\times}\text{:}\)
    🔗
    The results do hold! You’ll explore a bit more about when these results hold and when they don’t in the homework, and we’ll formalize them over the next two sections.
